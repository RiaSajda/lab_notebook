
# Class Hydrozoa

## Fire Corals
- Burning sensation cased by powerful batteries of stinging nematocysts on tentacles of polyps
- Stout feeding poly is encircled by 5-9 stinging polyps
- Polys gastric cavities are interconnected beneath the skeletal surface
- Coloration is generally tan mustard with white at the tips or edge of the skeletal structure:
  - Box fire coral is more tan to mustard brown with reddish to pink or lavender tints ![[Pasted image 20260518103132.png|432]]
- Growth patterns are branching, blade, ridge and box
## Lace Corals
- Polyps extend through pores in calcareous skeleton and form small cup like structures, giving a serrated appearance 
- Coloration is usually purple, burgundy or lavender at the base, fading to pink and white toward branch tips

# Class Anthozoa 
## Subclass Octocorallia
### Gorgonians (Octocorals)
- Central core is surrounded by rind, polyps are embedded in rind and extend through surface openings
- Shape of polyps apertures and rims around them is called calyces
- Most colonies are attached to substrate via a single holdfast at the base of a stem that may branch 
  - Branching patterns include unbranched, dichotomous, lateral, pinnate and interconnected![[Screenshot 2026-05-18 at 12.22.50 PM.png|374]]
### Telestaceans 
- Grow by extending a long terminal poly that produces a stem with short side branches tipped with daughter polys
  ![[Screenshot 2026-05-18 at 12.23.25 PM.png|396]]
  - Polyps are brilliant white
### Soft Corals
- Polyps are clumped on the branch tips, in small clusters and/or solitary polys on the branch surface
- Colony is composed of a rubbery, thick jelly like material with embedded skeletal spicules clearly visible
- Coloration range is pastel
## Subclass Hexacorallia
### Stony Corals
- Hard cup like structures on polyps are called coralites
- Grouping patterns include branching & pillar, encrusting, mound & boulder, brain, leaf, plate & sheet, fleshy and flowering & cup
- Corals that contribute substantial amounts of calcium carbonate to reef structure are called hermatypic, non-reef-building corals are called ahermatypic
  ![[Screenshot 2026-05-18 at 2.28.36 PM.png|395]]
## Order Antipatharia
- Polyps secrete a protein that hardens in concentric layers, forming branched/wire-like structures
- Branchlets are calles pinnules
- Each polyp has 6 small "non-retractable" tentacles 
- Coloration is usually gray, brown, rust red or green![[Screenshot 2026-05-18 at 3.11.53 PM.png]]
# Marine Plants & Algae
## Kingdom Protista
- Algae attach to the substrate by holdfast structures called rhizoids and runners connecting upright blades called rhizomes
- Upright part of the plant are called stalks rather than stems and blades instead of leaves
- Bulb-like structures containing gas are called bladders or floats
  - Flowering plants- class angiospermae. Most common is sea grass, grows in great beds in sand and sandy rubble adjacent to reefs
  - Brown algae- phylum phaeophyta. Best known are kelp and sargassum. Coloration ranges from pale creamy brown to yellow-brown, green-brown and dark brown, occasionally marked with shades of yellow, green and/or blue
  - Green algae- phylum chlorophyta. Many are calcareous and add significant amounts of calcium carbonate and white sand to and around the reefs. Growth patterns include single-celled bubble-like plants to fan-like blades, bristly brushes, hanging vines and serrated, upright blades. Coloration ranges from shades of pale mint green to bright kelly green, yellowish-green, brownish-green and dark green.
  - Red algae- phylum phodophyta. Most of the ones adding calcareous are encrusting. Appear to have coloration of dull red to lavender to purple of the rocky substrate. Reef crests exposed to vigorous currents are often cemented by calcareous red algae and are called algae ridges.
# Coral Health and Mortality
## Lesion Description
- Colour change- typically exemplified by a white colour
- Growth anomaly- deformed or enlarged polyps in relation to other polyps on same colony
- Skeletal damage- abrasion and/or removal or coralites and underlaying skeletal layers, caused by fish bites, physical impacts or environmental events
- Tissue loss-absence of tissue with or without intact skeleton
## Lesion Shape
- Annular- forms a ring
- Circular- forms a circle, but larger
- Irregular- lacking symmetry, not straight/smoothness/evenness/regularity
- Linear- forms a line
- Oblong- oval or elongate 
## Lesion Distribution
- Coalescing- multiple lesions growing together
- Diffuse- not concentrated or localised
- Focal- central lesion surrounded by tissue
- Multifocal- multiple lesions within colony surface, each surrounded by tissue
- Linear- beginning at the margin of the colony and advancing in a line or band
## Lesion Location
- Apical- apex or tip of colony or branch
- Basal/peripheral- base of colony
- Central/medial- lying or extending in middle of colony
## Black Band Disease (BBD)
- Crescent-shaped or circular shaped band of blackish filamentous material separating living, coloured coral tissue from white, exposed skeleton. 
- Bands often have white dusting of filamentous bacteria. Filaments are long and unbranched, intertwining to form a dense mat. 
  ![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 3.09.20 PM.png|326]]
  - Mat is anchored in living tissue but can easily be dislodged 
- Infection usually starts at the fringe of colony, in a surface depression or the edge or a previous injury
- Once established, progresses from fraction of an inch to an inch (2mm to 2cm) per day
- In western Atlantic, 24 species of massive and plating coral, 1 hydrozoan and 6 gorgonians are affected. Majority infected are boulder star coral and symmetrical brain coral (to date)
- In the red sea and Indo-Pacific, 26 coral species are affected. Staghorn coral and Elkhorn coral are not affected (to date)
- Caused by cyanobacteria, sulphur oxidising bacteria and sulphur reducing bacteria. Coral tissue is killed by hydrogen sulphide produced by these microorganisms
- Highly infectious and can kill small colonies in weeks/months while large colonies usually only lose up to half its tissue. Colonies are often reinfected as partial tissue loss affects corals ability to reproduce. Killed portion of coral is often colonised by organisms that erode the skeleton, limiting ability to recover
## White Band Disease (WBD)
- Affects branching corals, starts at the base of colony and progresses toward branch tips or begins in the middle of the colony, especially at point where branch begins
-  Colonies affected show a margin of slowly advancing tissue decay, exposing a contrasting bright white area of limestone skeleton next to dying tissue ![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 3.09.55 PM.png|265]]
- Disease causes tissue to peel away from the skeleton at a fairly uniform rate of 5mm per day. Exposed skeleton is colonised by algae within days
- Not entire colonies suffer complete mortality but recovered colonies are often reinfected
- Affected corals are Staghorn coral, fused Staghorn coral and Elkhorn coral. Observed throughout the wider Caribbean, Red Sea, Indo-Pacific and Gulf of Oman
- Host of bacteria, fungi and protozoans often found at infection site
### White Band Disease Type II (WBD II)
- Causes tissue death at a more rapid rate and is more virulent than white band disease
- Disease spreds from base of colony toward branch tips
- Appears as a band of bleached tissue varying from 2 to 20cm wide that separates normal pigmented tissue from white dead skeleton
- May be caused by bacteria of genus Vibrio, living on the surface mucus of bleached tissue 
- More common in Staghorn corals and can cause total colony mortality in a few days
## White Patch Disease (WPD)
- Affects Staghorn coral and Elkhorn coral
- Manifests as small patches of dead tissue surrounded by normal tissue. Patches can grow from 2 to 10cm in diameter 
- Patches are often located on upper surface of branches but can also be on lower surface of branches
  - Patches are bare of coral tissue
- Occurs throughout Caribbean 
- Possible cause is a bacteria found in the human gut for those affected in Florida keys. Elkhorn coral affected by bites from fishes such as threespot damselfish and yellowtail damselfish
## White Plague (WP)
- Tissue loss begins at base, margin of colony or prior infection site and spreads quickly
- Healthy tissue separated  from skeleton by a sharp line. A fine but distinct narrow band of bleached tissue separates normal, fully pigmented tissue from white, exposed skeleton![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 3.10.50 PM.png|310]]
- Tissue loss progresses up to 2cm per day
- Affects mountainous star coral and brain coral in the Caribbean, with colonies longer than a meter in diameter being killed in a week
### White Plague Type I
- Disease progresses by a few millimetres per day and can take months to kill and entire colony
- 13 coral species affected, primarily, starlet coral, cactus coral, boulder brain coral and mountainous star coral
### White Plague type II
- Disease spreads rapidly and can kill small corals in 1-2 days
- 40 coral species affected
  - Doesn’t affect Staghorn coral or Elkhorn coral
  - Elliptical star coral is particularly susceptible 
- Caused by a rod shaped bacterium, Aurantimonas coralicida
## Caribbean Yellow Band Disease 
- Begins as a pale yellow, circular blotch of tissue in the middle of colony or as a narrow band at the edge of a colony. Infected ares are surrounded by normal, dark green to brown tissue. Affected area is translucent 
  ![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 3.22.43 PM.png|175]]
- Disease progresses by 1cm per month
- Tissue mortality is restricted to 5 to 10cm irregular blotches and can years to kill a large colony
- Most commonly affected are lobed mountainous coral, boulder star coral, great star coral and boulder brain coral
- Associated with several species of vibrio bacteria
- Seen in Florida Keys, Curacao, Bonaire, Panama, Mexico and Puerto Rico
## Red Band Disease (RBD)
- Narrow band or line of filamentous cyanobacteria that advances slowly across surface of coral
  ![[Screenshot 2026-05-21 at 4.53.17 PM.png|296]]
- Appears to be caused by cyanobacterias Schizothrix and Spirulina but evidence showing greater correlation to Oscillatoria and Gloeotheca-Gloecapsa exists
- Observed in Bahamas, Curacao, Puerto Rico and Jamaica
### Red Band Disease 1 (RBD-1)
- Forms a distinct band separating living coral tissue and white skeleton 
- Advances a few millimetres per day
- Band is red to maroon while cyanobacterial filaments are loosely organised 
- Affects lettuce coral, boulder brain coral, cactus coral, blushing star coral and common seafan
- Observed in Belize
### Red Band Disease 2(RBD-2)
- Filaments spread like a net over surface during the day and ball up at the interface between living tissue and exposed skeleton at night
- Affects symmetrical brain coral, lobed mountainous star coral, boulder star coral, great star coral, mustard hill coral and massive star coral
- Observed in Bahamas and throughout the Caribbean
## Dark Spots Disease (DSD)
- Appears as circular to irregular areas of pale discoloured tissue with darkened polyps or spots in the middle of normal tissue or at the colony’s margin
  ![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 3.24.36 PM.png|248]]
- Observed in massive starlet coral, blushing star coral, mountainous star coral, boulder star coral, lettuce coral and pencil coral
  - Discoloured area on massive starlet coral may be pink, brown or blue while colonies with blueish hue may show signs of bleaching
  - Blushing star coral exhibits darkened polyps that are slightly depressed and smaller than normal polyps
  - Associated with bacteria vibrio Carchariae
  - Observed in Columbia, throughout the Caribbean, Cayman Islands and Bahamas
## Hyperplasma
- Area of accelerated growth of coral polyps, polyps are visible but appear exaggerated
  - Ridges and valleys on brain coral or circular polyps in star corals are enlarged and project above colony’s surface
- Results in coralite distortions and other malformations
## Neoplasm
- Irregular calcified mass of skeleton that projects above the colony surface
- Covered with undifferentiated tissue that lacks symbiotic algae and structural organisation of normal tissue, making individual polyps unrecognisable 
- Calicoblastic epithelial cells grow and multiply at a rapid rate causing progressive increase in size of tumour 
- Condition advances upward and outward as tissue in center of tumour dies
## Caribbean Ciliate Infection
- Appears as a diffuse black or gray band several mm to 2cm thick, separating healthy tissue from bare skeleton or a diffuse scattered patch
  - both patches have a salt-and-pepper speckled appearance caused by presence of ciliates![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 3.25.13 PM.png|261]]
- Infects more than 25 species of hard corals in 6 families, Acroporidae, Agaricidae, Astrocoeniidae, Faviidae, Meandrinidae and Poritidae
- Caused by a Folliculinid ciliate in the genus Hallofoliculina
- Observed Caribbean wide and particularly offshore, oceanic reefs rather than coastal, human influenced reefs
## Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease (SCTLD)
- Appears to emerge in one distinct are and then advances across the reed and to neighbouring downstream reefs
- Initially appears on edge of colony, base of coral or adjacent to previously killed, exposed skeletal patch and spreads upwards and outwards
  - Lesions can also begin as patches or blotches with intact tissue that can increase in size and fuse together as tissue loss progresses
- Tissue loss characterised by newly exposed white skeleton, with corallites and other skeletal features intact 
  ![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 3.25.41 PM.png|188]]![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 3.26.35 PM.png|276]]
- Affects over 30 species
  - Elliptical star coral, pillar coral, maze coral and smooth flower coral are usually the first to be infected and die
  - Brain corals are often affected weeks after disease emerges on reef
  - Boulder corals show signs of infection after initial coral species have been infected and eliminated from the reef
  - Lettuce corals are susceptible while mustard  hill corals have low susceptibility 
  - Branching Elkhorn and Staghorn coral and fused Staghorn coral aren't affected
- If maze corals, elliptical star corals and brain corals aren’t affected first while boulder corals are infected then its more likely to be white plague rather than SCTLD
- Caused by a pathogen and environmental stressors
  - Disease appears to be waterborne, with reservoirs of pathogens within sediment
  - Bacterias involved are vibrio, Arcobacter, Algiocola, Planktoalea, Rhodobacterales and Rhizobiales
  - Vibrio Coralliilyticus increases virulence and rate of spread of the disease and reduces effectiveness of antibiotic and probiotic treatments
- Observed in Miami, Florida and Caribbean 
- Early susceptible species are maze coral, pillar coral, flower coral, elliptical star coral and brain corals
  - Mortality observed within a week to month
- Linear tissue loss advances at a rate of 3-4cm per day
  - In some cases, multiple lesions appear on individual colonies, coalesce and continue to advance while others halt after variable amounts of time
  - Some corals survive initial lesion appeared but didnt enlarge, then new lesions appeared at a later date
## Coral Bleaching
- Bleaching results in loss of symbiotic algae contained within coral tissue or loss or photosynthetic pigments that give the coral its normal colour
- Coral appears lighter in colour, mottled or white
  ![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 3.27.06 PM.png|244]]
  - In massive starlet coral, partially bleached colonies may be a shade of blue or pink
- Caused by environmental stressors, especially elevated water temp and UV radiation
  - In a few cases vibrio bacteria and protozoans have been known to cause bleaching 
- Observed in puerto rico, virgin islands, eastern Caribbean, cayman islands, bonaire and curacao
## Gorgonian Diseases
- Octocorals are affected by black band disease, red band disease, aspergillosis, tumors and various invertebrate predators
  - BBD affects sea fans and sea plums
  - RBD affects sea fans
  - Aspergillosis is caused by soil fungus and results in tissue death and erosion of skeleton
- Disease forms a characteristic band, 1-20mm wide that progressively advances outward from either base of the colony or within blades at site of previous injuries
- Common sea fan and venus sea fan display one or more irregular patches that lack living tissue. These areas expand in size until they eventually result in holes on the blade![[Screenshot 2026-05-21 at 4.52.05 PM.png|250]]
- The living tissue surrounding these lesions becomes dark purple and develops spherical  nodules or galls.
  - Disease is verified by fine white fungal filaments adjacent to living tissue  
## Gorgonian predators 
- Snails feeding on gorgonians graze a trail as they consume tissue. These trails expose spicules in a line the width of the predators width                                       ![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 3.33.39 PM.png|163]]
- Other coral eating snails remain near the holdfast and leave being feeding scars that extend up the blade in narrow lines
## Bearded Fireworm Predation
- Bearded corals have a preference for branching corals and corals weakened by disease
  - Feed primarily on branch tips of staghorn coral, on raised knobs on elkhorn coral and on fire coral
## Short Coral Snail
- Short coral snail is found on over 20 species of stony corals and occasionally on soft corals
  - Large snails (5cm) can occasionally kill entire colonies![[Screenshot 2026-05-21 at 5.02.11 PM.png|339]]
- Corals affected are elkhorn coral, staghorn coral, massive starlet coral and lettuce coral
  - Most common on lobed star coral
- Observed in florida and puerto rico
## Coral Predation
- Excavators remove coral tissue and a substantial portion of the underlying skeleton
- Scrapers remove tissue and only the upper surfaces of the coral calices
- Browsers and grazers remove soft parts without significant damage to the skeleton
- Some invertebrates also feed exclusively on coral mucus, without causing gross visible lesions
## Parrotfish Focused Biting
- Parrotfish take small bites over the surface of coral heads, creating pared grazing scars, this is called "Spot Biting"
- "Focused Biting" occurs when large parrotfish return to bite the same coral head, producing large lesions on the colony's surface
- Damage by fish bites generally removes both living tissue and top layers of skeleton
  - Disease leaves the coral skeleton intact
- Seen in lobed star coral, mountainous star coral and boulder brain coral
- Affected colonies appear to have been physically abraded, with damage extending 2-10cm wide strips
  - Parrotfish bite at prominent knobs on mountainous star coral leaving golf ball-sized or larger lesions
  - Boulder brain coral grazing begins at the edge of the colony and progresses forming a uniform strip                                                                                                                                          ![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 4.32.32 PM.png|198]]
  - Plating and branching type corals are usually bitten around their edges and branch tips
  - Observed in most coral species in the Caribbean 
## Damselfish Predation
- Damselfish take numerous small bites over the surface of coral colonies, killing an area of living polyps that is colonised by tufts of algae
- Fish removes many polyps forming numerous chimneys on a single colony
## Sponge and Tunicate Overgrowth
- Sponges colonise dead areas at the margins of living tissue, or substrate adjacent to colonies
  - Some sponge species secrete an acid that lets them bore directly into a corals skeleton, compromising the structure till sections break away from the colony
  - Other competitive  sponges and tunicates infiltrate corals by injecting compounds that kill the living tissue of stony corals
  - Encrusting sponge species kill the living tissue, excavate the upper layers of the coral skeleton and create a series of cavities and tunnels within the colonies that they bore into
# Reproduction and Growth of Stony Corals and Gorgonians
- Fertilised eggs develop into tiny free living offspring known as planula larvae
  - Corals that develop planula larvae internally are called brooders
  - Species that release eggs, sperm or both into the water are called broadcast spawners
    - Gonochoric species with separate male and female colonies release wither sperm or eggs that cross fertilise in the water column![[Screenshot 2026-05-21 at 5.01.07 PM.png|390]]
    - Hermaphrodites package sperm and eggs into pink gamete bundles that are expelled to the caprice of the currents
    
  ![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 5.31.10 PM.png|178]]![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 5.31.54 PM.png|309]]
- Planula larvae can remain viable for weeks or months while riding the currents in the open water
- Polyps are produced via asexual reproduction
  - New polyps can erupt between or bud from established polyps, a process called extra-tentacular budding
  - As the polyps grows, it can divide or split off into one or more polyps, a process called fission/intra-tentacular budding                                                ![[Screenshot 2026-05-21 at 5.03.51 PM.png|244]]
  - New coral colonies also form by fragmentation when pieces of colonies break off due to strong currents, storms, anchor damage, boat grounding, careless divers or as the result of erosion or predators 
    - If the fragment is of sufficient size and settle on appropriate substrate, they establish distinct new colonies that are genetically identical to the parent colonies
