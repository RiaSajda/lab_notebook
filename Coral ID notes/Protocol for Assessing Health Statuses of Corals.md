# Diseased 
- Refers to stony coral tissue loss disease
Disease signs:
- Usually focal or multifocal lesions of rapid tissue loss
  - More susceptible species will have acute tissue loss (lesion progresses quickly)
  - Less susceptible species may have subacute tissue loss (disease lesion spreads slower)
- Bare white skeleton is exposed closest to disease margin. Algae has likely started growing on the dead, white skeleton furthest away from the disease line
  - Very rapid tissue loss in highly susceptible species may result in little algal growth, so the dead skeleton will still look very white
  - Subacute tissue loss may result in more algae growth![[Screenshot 2026-05-27 at 10.12.57 AM.png]]
- SSIDs display different disease signs than most other coral species. They may have some purple discolouration, and overall splotchiness and tissue loss that looks more like webbing and multifocal lesions (may have both) instead of a discrete, focal lesion
  - If there is discolouration, only call diseases if there is also tissue loss
# Diseased_Other
- May include black band disease (BBD) or white band disease (WBD)![[Screenshot 2026-05-27 at 10.13.26 AM.png]]
- If you think you can ID the disease, put it in notes
# Predation/Health Issues
- Predation can look very similar to some of these but it is normal, and corals experiencing predation should be recorded as healthy
- Corallivorous snails can do major damage to corals. They eat corals in groups and can leave a band of dead tissue that may look similar to SCTLD lesions. Sometimes these snails can be quite camouflaged.![[Screenshot 2026-05-27 at 10.14.00 AM.png]]
- Fish predation. Small discrete bite marks that leave dead skeleton behind
  - Usually smaller marks of white, sporadic, may not be covering the whole colony. In PASTs, usually at the top of the 'hills'
- Competition/abrasion
  - Boring sponges or soft corals will grow in/around coral and there may be abrasion spots or tissue damage near this overgrowth. 
# Colour Loss Bleaching - CLB
- Coral colonies may be partially or totally bleached
  - Record % of bleaching (estimate of affected % of the whole colony)
- Completely bleached colonies will appear fully white but their tissues are still alive
- Usually coincides with high sea temperatures or other environmental stressors 
  - Usually occurs in waves where many species are affected simultaneously 
- Bleaching and paling will usually occur on the top side of the coral
  - Not always the case, its usually at the top as its the most exposed to the sun and the most sensitive
  - Also the reason for collecting coral samples from the side of the coral
# Colour Loss Paling - CLP
- Sometimes colonies will appear lighter in colour before bleaching completely, or part of the colony will pale and part of it will be completely bleached ![[Screenshot 2026-05-27 at 10.14.53 AM.png|268]]
  - Record % of paling (estimate of affected % of whole colony)
  - Also associated with high temperatures
# Discolouration 
- Usually multifocal (or focal) patches of abnormal coloration on the colony that doesn't fit any of the above descriptions                                     ![[Screenshot 2026-05-27 at 10.15.07 AM.png]]
# Estimating % Tissue Loss
- Estimate percent tissue loss (including old mortality) of colony. Based on size of the coral colony 
1. Record % live tissue remaining of the entire skeleton. Estimate to the nearest 5
- Take into account existing old mortality in your first tissue estimate 
  - This way, you should be able to determine the % tissue remaining at each individual time point. However, if you run into tissues or are missing photos of the entire colony, you should refer back to the initial photo
- So one on left starts at 98% tissue and on the right is 5%
- Do not include our sample punches in tissue loss estimates
1. Two independent observers record estimated TL
2. If the two estimates are within 10%, average the two. If the difference is >10%, review with third observer![[Screenshot 2026-05-27 at 10.15.23 AM.png]]
